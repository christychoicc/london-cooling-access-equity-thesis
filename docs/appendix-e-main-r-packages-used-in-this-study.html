Appendix E Main R-packages used in this study

Table 6.8: Summary of main R-packages used for the analysis of this study. R-packages with (*) are installed remotely, while others are installed from CRAN in R.
R-package Description Support
car To assess multicollinearity of variables using variance inflation factors (VIF). MacLachlan and Dennett (2022)
corrplot To visualise correlation matrices for examining correlations relationships between modelling variables. Wei and Simko (2024)
dplyr For data manipulation, including filtering, joining, selecting and transforming datasets. MacLachlan and Dennett (2022)
ggplot2 For creating graphical and mapping figure outputs. Wickham et al. (2016)
gtfstools To read, filter and manipulate transport timetable feed data (GTFS) prior to computation of r5r routing. Pereira and Herszenhut (2023)
INLA * To perform fast and accurate model fitting for approximate Bayesian inference. R-INLA (no date), Gómez-Rubio (2020)
MASS To fit Negative Binomial Generalised Linear Models. Venables and Ripley (2002)
r5r To build multimodal network and compute travel routes and travel time matrices efficiently between origin-destination pairs. Pereira et al. (2021)
rJavaEnv To configure a Java environment in R sessions for computing r5r (r5r is package is Java dependent). Kotov (2024)
sf For storing and manipulating data. MacLachlan and Dennett (2022)
spdep To create spatial weight matrices and calculate Global Moran’s I and Local Indicators of Spatial Association statistics to test spatial autocorrelation. Bivand and Wong (2018)
terra To read raster data and ensure consistent coordinate reference systems (CRS) between datasets. Hijmans et al. (2026)
tidyr To tidy and clean data. Wickham et al. (2025)
UK2GTFS * To convert rail timetable data into GTFS format for r5r routing. Verduzco Torres and McArthur (2024)
